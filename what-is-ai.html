What is AI?

A simple definition

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is technology that allows computers to perform tasks that normally need human thinking — like recognising speech, understanding images, or predicting what word comes next in a sentence.

How does it learn?

Most modern AI learns from huge amounts of examples (called data), finding patterns instead of following rules a programmer typed out one by one.

For example, an AI that recognises cats in photos isn't given a rulebook describing whiskers and pointy ears. Instead, it's shown thousands of labelled photos of cats and non-cats, and it gradually learns to spot the patterns that tell them apart — the same way you'd get better at spotting cats the more you saw.

Different types of AI you'll come across

Not all AI works the same way. A few common types you'll see mentioned:

Machine learning — the AI improves by looking at examples, like the cat example above. This is behind things like spam filters and recommendation systems.

Generative AI — creates new content, like text, images, or music, based on patterns it learned from existing examples. Tools like chatbots and AI art generators fall into this category.

Where you already use it

Voice assistants, spelling suggestions, video recommendations, and photo filters are everyday examples of AI already in your pocket.
